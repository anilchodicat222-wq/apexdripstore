In aim-heavy games the mouse is the piece of gear you notice most. The best competitive mice are not the ones with the most buttons or the highest DPI number; they're the ones that disappear in your hand so your aim is consistent from one match to the next.

What matters for competitive play

Shape and grip come first

Hold your hand flat and measure from the wrist crease to the tip of your middle finger. Under about 17 cm, smaller symmetrical shapes usually suit claw and fingertip grips. Above about 19 cm, larger ergonomic shapes are more comfortable for palm grip. In between, try both styles if you can. A shape that fits your grip beats any spec improvement.

Weight

Lighter mice are easier to start and stop, which helps with flicks and micro-adjustments. Most competitive mice now sit between about 55 and 65 g. Below that, some players find the mouse harder to control precisely. If you've used a heavy mouse for years, expect a week of adjustment.

Sensor, DPI and polling rate

Every mouse in this guide has a sensor that tracks accurately at the speeds real players move. Ignore marketing about maximum DPI: most professionals play between 400 and 1600 DPI. Higher polling rates (4K or 8K Hz) can make cursor motion feel smoother on high-refresh monitors, but they cost battery life and need a capable PC. 1000 Hz is still perfectly competitive.

Wireless is now the default

Modern 2.4 GHz wireless from the major brands performs like a cable, and removing drag is one of the most noticeable upgrades you can make. Keep the receiver close to the mouse, ideally on the desk with an extension adapter.

Buying advice

  1. Pick a shape for your grip, then choose the lightest mouse in that shape you can afford.
  2. Get a mouse pad that matches your style. Control pads slow the mouse for steadier tracking; speed and glass pads like the Redragon PG1M suit fast, flick-heavy play. See our setup essentials guide.
  3. Set it and forget it. Choose one DPI, turn off pointer acceleration in Windows, and keep the same in-game sensitivity for weeks. Consistency improves aim more than new gear.
  4. Buy from your local store so that returns are easy if the shape doesn't suit you. Every card on APEXDRIP shows the country of the store.

Quick answers

Do I need an 8K polling rate?

No. It can feel smoother on 240 Hz+ monitors, but 1000 Hz is reliable, uses less battery and is what most players still use.

Wired or wireless for competitive play?

Either is fine with today's top mice. Wireless removes cable drag; wired is cheaper and never needs charging.
