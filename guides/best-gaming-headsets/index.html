A good headset does two jobs: it places every footstep and gunshot where it really happened, and it keeps you comfortable for hours. Most of the differences between headsets come from fit, tuning and microphone quality rather than headline features.

What to look for

Comfort is non-negotiable

Clamping force, weight and earcup depth decide whether you'll still enjoy a headset after three hours. Lighter headsets with suspension or memory-foam headbands, like the Logitech G733 and HyperX Cloud III, suit long sessions. If you wear glasses, softer earpads help.

Wired or wireless?

Wired headsets give the best sound per rupee, dollar or pound and never run out of battery. 2.4 GHz wireless removes the cable without adding noticeable lag; Bluetooth is convenient for phones but adds delay, so use it for music and calls rather than competitive games.

Spatial audio

Virtual surround and spatial audio (such as DTS Headphone:X or 7.1 modes) can make positioning feel wider in some games. Many competitive players turn it off and rely on the game's own audio engine. Treat it as a bonus, not a reason to buy.

Microphone quality

If you play in a squad, the mic matters as much as the speakers. Detachable boom mics with noise filtering keep your voice clear. Streamers often pair a headset with a separate microphone; see our creator desk guide.

Buying advice

  1. Set a comfort budget first. If you have a large head or wear glasses, prioritise adjustable headbands and deep earpads.
  2. Match the connection to your platform. Check console compatibility on the product page; some wireless dongles are PC/PlayStation only.
  3. Use the game's own audio settings (often called "headphones" mode) before reaching for surround features.
  4. Keep volume moderate. Hearing damage builds up slowly; good positioning lets you play quieter.
