A good mechanical keyboard no longer costs a fortune. The budget end of the market now offers hot-swappable switches, compact layouts and even magnetic Hall-effect switches that were reserved for premium boards a couple of years ago. The hard part is knowing which features change how a keyboard feels in a match and which ones only look good on a spec sheet.

This guide explains what to prioritise, compares the keyboards we list at APEXDRIP, and points you to the right regional store so you see prices in your own currency.

What actually matters on a budget

1. Layout first, everything else second

The layout decides how much room your mouse gets, and that affects aim more than any switch. A full-size board with a number pad is about 44 cm wide; a tenkeyless (TKL) board drops the number pad; a 75% layout tucks the function row and arrows into a tighter frame; a 65% layout drops the function row entirely. If you play shooters at low sensitivity, a 65% or 75% board gives you several extra centimetres of swipe space. If you also work in spreadsheets, a TKL or full-size board will annoy you less.

2. Switch type

  • Linear switches go straight down with no bump. Most competitive players prefer them because double-taps and quick strafes feel predictable.
  • Tactile switches have a small bump that tells your finger the key has registered. They suit mixed gaming and typing.
  • Magnetic Hall-effect switches measure how far a key is pressed with a magnet and sensor. That allows adjustable actuation and rapid trigger, where a key resets the instant you start lifting it. In tactical shooters this makes counter-strafing noticeably snappier.

3. Wired vs wireless

Wired boards are cheaper for the same build quality and never need charging. Modern 2.4 GHz wireless is fast enough for competitive play, but at budget prices you usually trade something else, such as case quality or switch choice, to get it.

Buying advice

  1. Measure your desk. If your mouse hits the keyboard during big swipes, choose 65% or 75% before anything else.
  2. Play tactical shooters? Shortlist Hall-effect boards like the Kreo Hive75 HE or Redragon K769 HE. For MOBAs, MMOs and general use, rapid trigger matters much less.
  3. Check your region. Buy from the store for your own country to get local warranty, returns and pricing. Every APEXDRIP card shows the country and currency.
  4. Look at keycap legends. Budget boards sometimes use thin ABS keycaps that shine over time. That's easy to fix later with a new set if the board is hot-swappable.
  5. Don't pay for features you won't use. Screens, dials and macro rows are fun, but switches, layout and build quality decide whether you'll still like the board in two years.

Quick answers

Is a Hall-effect keyboard worth it for casual players?

Not essential. If you mostly play single-player games or MOBAs, a regular mechanical board with switches you enjoy is the better value. Rapid trigger shines in games where you constantly stop and start moving.

Can a budget keyboard last?

Yes. Switches are rated for tens of millions of presses. The parts that wear first are keycaps and stabilisers, which are cheap to replace.
